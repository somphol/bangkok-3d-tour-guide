# Glossary

A short guide to the Thai and Bangkok terms used in the experience. Royal and monastic titles are
simplified for a general audience.

| Term | Thai | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wat** | วัด | A Buddhist temple complex; usually includes an ordination hall, monks’ quarters and stupas. |
| **Prang** | ปรางค์ | A tall, tower-like Khmer-style sanctuary, often with a square plan and a spire. Wat Arun’s central prang is the model for the porcelain-clad tower in the scene. |
| **Chedi** | เจดีย์ | A stupa or reliquary monument. Bell-shaped chedis may enshrine relics; the Golden Mount’s summit chedi is the most prominent example here. |
| **Ubosot** | อุโบสถ | The ordination hall of a temple, usually the most sacred building, used for monastic ceremonies. |
| **Wihan** | วิหาร | An assembly or image hall, often housing a principal Buddha image. |
| **Mondop** | มณฑป | A square pavilion, sometimes with a spire, used to house a sacred object or image. |
| **Chofah** | ช่อฟ้า | The curved finial at the end of a Thai temple roof ridge, usually gold. |
| **Naga** | นาค | A serpent deity; naga balustrades and stair rails guard many temple entrances. |
| **Yaksha** | ยักษ์ | A guardian giant; painted or ceramic yaksha figures often flank temple gates. |
| **Rattanakosin** | รัตนโกสินทร์ | The historic core of Bangkok and the name of the current Thai kingdom (since 1782). |
| **Chao Phraya** | เจ้าพระยา | The river that runs through Bangkok; the city’s original highway and the axis of this guide. |
| **Tha Tien** | ท่าตีียน | A pier on the east bank opposite Wat Arun, used for the cross-river ferry and Wat Pho. |
| **Tha Chang** | ท่าช้าง | The “Elephant Pier” near the Grand Palace, historically used for royal processions. |
| **Loy Krathong** | ลอยกระทง | A festival in which small decorated floats are set on water; the Golden Mount also has a red-cloth procession. |
| **Emerald Buddha** | พระแก้วมรกต | The revered jade image at Wat Phra Kaew inside the Grand Palace; only the king may touch it. |
| **Phra Kaew** | พระแก้ว | Short name for the Emerald Buddha and the temple that houses it. |
| **Chakri** | จักรี | The current royal dynasty, founded in 1782. |
| **Pratunam** | ประตูน้ำ | The garment and market district around Baiyoke Tower II. |
| **Baiyoke** | ใบหยก | The tower complex in Pratunam; Baiyoke Tower II opened in 1997. |
| **THB** | บาท | Thai baht, the currency used in the visitor tips. Prices are a 2026 snapshot, not live data. |

## Pronunciation notes

- *Wat* is a short, unaspirated “wat”, not “what”.
- *Chedi* is roughly “chay-dee”; *prang* rhymes with “hang”.
- Thai names in this project use the common English transliteration, which is not a phonetic standard.

## Temple etiquette in one line

Cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes where indicated, keep voices low, do not point feet at a Buddha
image, and check the current dress code before entering an active monastery.
